# Ratios: Valuation and Quality

Status: implemented 2026-09-13. Specified 2026-09-13.

Two more pillars, computed nightly from the line items fundamentals ingest stores and the bars
price ingest stores: **Valuation** (is it cheap for what it is?) and **Quality** (is it a good
business?). The screen stops being a momentum ranking. Standing decisions live in `DESIGN.md`; the
facts this reads are specified in `docs/specs/2026-09-06-fundamentals-ingest.md`, and the run it
extends in `docs/specs/2026-09-05-scoring.md`.

Scope: **ten derived metrics, the rules that decide which period and which peers each one is
computed against, and the changes that let one night score three pillars.** Nothing new is
ingested. No ratio is stored as a fact (fundamentals D3). No alert is evaluated: every run still
writes `emits_alerts = false`.

---

## 1. What this has to satisfy

1. **The screen has to measure what `DESIGN.md` describes.** Three nights of v1 (2026-09-09 to
   09-11) scored 1,499 securities on Momentum alone, so the top of the screen is the set of stocks
   whose price rose most within their sector — one pillar of five, under a blended score's name.
2. **Every ratio traces back to visible raw inputs** — including *which period* it was computed
   from, because the same line item means different things in different periods.
3. **A ratio must never be plausible and wrong.** Fundamentals F7 named the shape: a read taking
   "the latest value per metric" pairs 2025 revenue with 2023 interest expense and produces a
   reasonable-looking number. This cycle finds four more routes to that shape (F1, F3, F4, F5), and
   each one is answered by a rule below rather than by care.
4. **What follows gets cheaper.** The UI swap draws `Row.p`, five pillar slots, and alerting diffs
   snapshots. Both are better built against three real pillars than one, and a
   `scoring_logic_version` bump costs nothing while nothing reads it for alerts.

## 2. Findings

Measured on production on 2026-09-11 and 09-12, after three nights: 1,504 securities, ~347k facts.

**F1 — TTM can be assembled for most of the universe, but not by taking the latest four
quarters.** Securities holding four quarterly facts whose newest ends at most 135 days before
`as_of` and which span about nine months:

| item | clean TTM | four held, **one missing between** | fewer than four |
|---|---|---|---|
| `revenue` | 1,430 | 16 | 3 |
| `operating_cash_flow` | 1,430 | 14 | 2 |
| `net_income` | 1,428 | 16 | 5 |
| `depreciation_amortisation` | 1,332 | 14 | 10 |
| `capital_expenditure` | 1,316 | 33 | 9 |
| `ebit` | 1,304 | 16 | 4 |
| `interest_expense` | 1,223 | 35 | 32 |

The middle column is the trap. "The latest four" for those securities spans fifteen months, and
summing it produces a year a third too large. Consecutiveness has to be checked, not assumed.

**F2 — an annual figure is structurally eight months old.** Days between 2026-09-11 and the latest
`net_income` period held:

| basis | median | p90 | p99 | max | coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| quarterly | 73 | 73 | 164 | 346 | 96% within 135 days |
| annual | 254 | 254 | 376 | 680 | 99.8% within 15 months |

A calendar-year filer's FY2025 stays its newest annual until spring. Scoring on annual figures
alone values today's price against earnings most of a year old, and moves once a year by a whole
10-K.

**F3 — Yahoo reports capital expenditure as a negative number, for every security.** On the
latest quarterly value, `capital_expenditure` is negative for all 1,414 securities that report it.
The textbook `FCF = OCF − capex` therefore adds capex back and inflates every free cash flow. The
signs the absence rules (D5) depend on:

| item | negative | zero | positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stockholders_equity` | 62 | 0 | 1,442 |
| `ebit` | 91 | 0 | 1,282 |
| `tax_provision` | 119 | 2 | 1,350 |
| `interest_expense` | 2 | 8 | 1,338 |
| `depreciation_amortisation` | 10 | 0 | 1,403 |

**F4 — Yahoo restates share counts for splits, so adjusting them again double-counts.** Six
securities have a `split` action since 2026-08-01. For the five genuine splits, the stored
`shares_outstanding` at the June quarter is already on the post-split basis: multiplied by today's
close it gives APH $206.9bn, MNST $85.0bn, IESC $13.8bn, RUSHA $5.6bn and SFBS $4.6bn — each the
company's actual size, and each half (or two-thirds) of what multiplying by the split ratio again
would claim. The sixth, REZI at `1.437`, is a spin-off price factor rather than a change in share
count, and applying it would be wrong for a different reason. Restatement is live rather than a
first-night artifact: `shares_basic_avg` already carries second observations for the same periods.
The one timing point is APH: split effective 2026-09-03, restated in the 09-09 fetch.

Recorded plainly because this cycle's first draft of the rule assumed the opposite: it multiplied
shares by every split after the count's date, which would have doubled all five caps.

**F5 — a handful of facts are not in the price's currency.** Every security prices in USD. Two
securities hold AUD facts and one holds EUR facts, 14 facts in all. A USD price over a EUR figure
is a ratio of the right order of magnitude and the wrong value.

**F6 — where a ratio means nothing is decided by industry, not sector.** Financial Services (238)
holds banks (`banks-regional` 92, `banks-diversified` 5), insurers (53 across five `insurance-*`
industries, brokers aside) and `mortgage-finance` (1) — businesses whose debt is inventory, for which EV/EBITDA,
ROIC, debt/equity, interest cover and gross margin measure nothing — beside
`financial-data-stock-exchanges` (11), `insurance-brokers` (9) and `asset-management` (28), which
are ordinary businesses. Real Estate (112) is 104 REITs, whose net income is depressed by
depreciation on property that generally appreciates, and 8 companies that are not REITs. Two
industries mix both kinds: `credit-services` puts Visa and Mastercard beside Capital One and
Synchrony, and `capital-markets` puts Goldman Sachs beside exchanges and brokers.

**F7 — three parts of scoring assume one pillar, and would be quietly wrong with three.**

- `score_pillar(expected=len(CODES))` divides by every metric in the pillar, so a metric that does
  not apply to a security reads as missing: a bank with a perfect ROE would show Quality coverage
  of 1/4.
- `blend` takes `min_coverage` over the pillars *present*, so a security with no fundamentals at
  all is written with `min_coverage = 1.0` beside complete three-pillar scores.
- `peers.resolve` enforces `MIN_PEERS` on how many securities a group holds, not on how many
  produced a given metric. Real Estate holds 112; its non-REIT `earnings_yield` bucket would hold 8.

**F8 — `metric_daily` has room for one fact.** `fundamental_fact_id` is commented "null for
price-derived metrics", and the schema spec's intent was a literal chain from percentile to stored
response. A TTM ratio rests on 4 to 16 facts.

## 3. Decisions

**D1 — One cycle, both pillars, a small metric set.** Most of the work is machinery both pillars
need: TTM assembly, the basis rule, balance items at a date, the share-count guard, the currency
rule, industry applicability and three-pillar scoring. Doing it once means one logic-version bump
rather than two. The metric set is what the stored line items support, which rules out three of
`DESIGN.md`'s examples regardless of scope: **PEG** needs forward growth estimates and nothing
stores one; **gross margin trend** and **earnings consistency** would rest on at most four annual
points (fundamentals D10). They are deferred, not dropped (§9).

**D2 — Yields, not multiples.** A multiple changes meaning through zero: a P/E of −5 is not cheaper
than one of 10, and sorting multiples puts loss-makers at the top of Valuation. A yield is monotonic
through zero, so a loss-maker ranks as expensive, which is what it is. Valuation stores
`earnings_yield` rather than P/E, and `ebitda_ev` rather than EV/EBITDA.

**D3 — The metrics.**

Valuation, `cadence = 'daily'` because the denominator moves with the price:

| code | value | higher is better |
|---|---|---|
| `earnings_yield` | `net_income` / market cap | yes |
| `ebitda_ev` | (`ebit` + `depreciation_amortisation`) / enterprise value | yes |
| `fcf_yield` | (`operating_cash_flow` **+** `capital_expenditure`) / market cap | yes |
| `book_yield` | `stockholders_equity` / market cap — a substitute only (D4) | yes |
| `ffo_yield` | (`net_income` + `depreciation_amortisation`) / market cap — a substitute only (D4) | yes |

Quality, `cadence = 'quarterly'` because only a new fact moves it:

| code | value | higher is better |
|---|---|---|
| `roic` | `ebit` × (1 − t) / (`stockholders_equity` + `total_debt` − `cash_and_equivalents`) | yes |
| `roe` | `net_income` / `stockholders_equity` — a substitute only (D4) | yes |
| `gross_margin` | `gross_profit` / `revenue` | yes |
| `debt_to_equity` | `total_debt` / `stockholders_equity` | **no** |
| `interest_cover` | `ebit` / `interest_expense` | yes |

Flow items are read on the ratio's basis (D6) and balance items at a date (D7). Enterprise value is
market cap + `total_debt` − `cash_and_equivalents`. The tax rate t is `tax_provision` /
`pretax_income` on the same basis, clamped to [0, 0.5] when `pretax_income` > 0, and 0 otherwise:
a negative provision is a tax benefit, not a negative rate. `fcf_yield` *adds* capital expenditure
because Yahoo reports it negative (F3). `cash_and_equivalents` is used rather than
`cash_and_short_term_investments` for its coverage (1,502 securities against 1,378), at the cost of
overstating enterprise value for cash-rich companies holding short-term investments (§10).

**D4 — Substitution by industry.** Applicability reads the security's level-2 industry node;
percentiles stay at sector level (scoring D5 is unchanged). Every security belongs to one class,
matched on industry code:

| class | industries | Valuation | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| balance sheet | `banks-*`, `insurance-*` except `insurance-brokers`, `mortgage-finance` | `earnings_yield`, `book_yield` | `roe` |
| REIT | `reit-*` | `ffo_yield`, `ebitda_ev`, `fcf_yield` | `roic`, `debt_to_equity`, `interest_cover` |
| standard | everything else | `earnings_yield`, `ebitda_ev`, `fcf_yield` | `roic`, `gross_margin`, `debt_to_equity`, `interest_cover` |

A metric outside a security's class is **inapplicable**, which is not the same as absent: it is not
computed, not written, and not counted in coverage (D10). `book_yield` and `roe` substitute rather
than join every class because, for an ordinary business, ROE and ROIC measure one profitability
twice, and a pillar average would count it double.

`credit-services` and `capital-markets` are `standard`. That treats Capital One like Visa and
Goldman Sachs like an exchange, and it is accepted for v2 as a named imprecision (§10) rather than
answered with an override list on the first attempt: an override list is a second classification to
maintain beside `data/universe.csv`, which is already the reviewed one.

**D5 — Absence rules.** A value that cannot be honestly computed is absent: dropped from its pillar's
average, never imputed (`DESIGN.md`).

- Market cap absent or ≤ 0 → every Valuation ratio is absent.
- Enterprise value ≤ 0 → `ebitda_ev` is absent.
- Invested capital ≤ 0 → `roic` is absent.
- `stockholders_equity` ≤ 0 → `debt_to_equity`, `roe` and `book_yield` are absent. A negative
  denominator would give a heavily indebted company a negative debt/equity and rank it the least
  leveraged. 62 securities today (F3).
- `interest_expense` ≤ 0 → `interest_cover` is absent. A debt-free company loses the metric, and
  the signal survives in the pillar only while Yahoo still reports `total_debt` as 0; where Yahoo
  stops publishing the series instead (as for MNST, whose `total_debt` ends 2025-03-31),
  `debt_to_equity` and `ebitda_ev` are absent too under the 456-day bound, and a zero is not
  imputed.
- `depreciation_amortisation` < 0 → every ratio using it is absent (10 securities; not
  interpretable).
- `capital_expenditure` > 0 → `fcf_yield` is absent. F3 found none; a positive value would mean the
  convention changed, which should surface as lost coverage rather than silently halve FCF.
- `revenue` ≤ 0 → `gross_margin` is absent.
- Any input unavailable under D6, D7, D8 or D9 → the ratio is absent.

Numerators may be negative — negative earnings, EBIT, free cash flow — and the ratio stands. That is
the case D2 exists for.

**D6 — One basis per ratio: consecutive TTM, else one annual period, else absent.** For each ratio,
over the flow items it reads:

1. **TTM.** The newest quarter end E such that every flow input holds quarterly values at **the same**
   four `period_end`s ending at E, each successive gap is 80 to 100 days, and `as_of` − E ≤ 135 days.
   Each input's value is the sum of its four.
2. **Else annual.** The newest annual `period_end` A with `as_of` − A ≤ 456 days at which every flow
   input holds a `period_type = 'A'` value.
3. **Else the ratio is absent.**

All flow inputs to one ratio share one basis and one end date. That is the rule F1 and fundamentals
F7 both call for: fresh inputs from different periods make a wrong ratio, so freshness is decided per
ratio rather than per fact. If one capex quarter is missing, the whole of `fcf_yield` moves to the
annual basis, not only its capex. Quarterly and annual values at one `period_end` stay distinct
(fundamentals D6): a fiscal Q4 is never read as its year, nor a year as a quarter.

The bounds: 135 days holds the latest quarter for 96% of the universe (F2's p90 is 73, leaving a
reporting lag's margin); 80 to 100 days admits every fiscal quarter of a 52/53-week year; 456 days
(15 months) holds the latest annual for 99.8%. They are **constants in code**, because changing one
changes what a metric means, and so bumps `scoring_logic_version` — which changing a weight never
does.

**D7 — Balance items are read at a date, and Valuation denominators are current.**
`stockholders_equity`, `total_debt`, `cash_and_equivalents` and `shares_outstanding` describe a
moment, so a quarterly and an annual value at one `period_end` are the same figure and either label
is read.

- `roic` and `roe` read their balance items at their basis end date, E or A from D6, so a return is
  measured on the capital of the period that earned it. A balance item missing at that date makes the
  ratio absent.
- `debt_to_equity` has no flow. It takes the newest date D with `as_of` − D ≤ 135 days at which both
  items are held, else the newest such date ≤ 456 days.
- **Market cap and enterprise value are current.** They take the newest held values ≤ 456 days old,
  whatever the numerator's basis — a trailing yield is today's price over past earnings. The debt and
  cash in enterprise value are read at one date.

**D8 — Market cap takes Yahoo's share count as it stands, and is absent for a week after a split.**
Market cap = close × `shares_outstanding`. The close is the raw close of the latest bar visible under
the run's cutoff at or before `as_of` — the bar momentum already reads, and unchanged by total-return
adjustment, which is anchored at the present (scoring D4). **No split arithmetic is applied to share
counts**: per F4, Yahoo restates them, and a split factor on top double-counts.

The unsafe window is the gap before Yahoo restates. While `as_of` falls within **7 days** of a split's
`effective_date` (`effective_date` ≤ `as_of` < `effective_date` + 7), market cap is absent, so the
security's Valuation is absent that night. Splits are read under the same visibility cutoff as bars,
which keeps the rule reproducible. The window fails in the safe direction — a missing Valuation, never
a wrong one — and its length rests on one timing observation (§10). The close itself is also bounded,
to 7 days before `as_of` (amendment A5, §12).

**D9 — A fact not in the security's currency is not an input.** Every fact a ratio reads must carry
`currency` equal to `security.currency`, or that input is unavailable and the ratio absent (F5). No
conversion: a rate is a second series with its own point-in-time problem, for three securities.

**D10 — Coverage is measured against applicable metrics, and there is no floor at scoring time.**
`score_pillar`'s `expected` becomes the number of that pillar's metrics applicable to the security's
class (D4): a bank's Quality expects one. A pillar with at least one present metric is scored, as
today. `DESIGN.md` places the floor at alerting — *"coverage is stored alongside every pillar score
and alerting is gated on a coverage floor"* — and this cycle keeps it there.

**D11 — A weighted pillar that produced nothing counts as coverage 0.** `blend`'s `min_coverage` is
taken over every pillar the weight version weights positively, with an absent one at 0. A security
scored on Momentum alone is therefore written with `min_coverage = 0` rather than 1.0 (F7). The blended
score itself is unchanged: it still normalises over the pillars present, as `blend` documents, so an
absent pillar does not drag it towards zero. Only the column that says how much to trust it changes.

**D12 — The peer floor applies per metric.** `MIN_PEERS` is checked on the number of securities that
produced a metric within a peer group, not on the group's membership. A bucket under 20 falls back to
market level for that metric alone, and `metric_daily.fallback_level` and
`snapshot_daily.worst_fallback_level` record it, as they already exist to. Real Estate's 8 non-REITs
are ranked against the market on `earnings_yield` and `gross_margin`, and against their sector on
anything with enough producers.

**D13 — Logic version v2 and weight version v2, equal thirds.** Migration 022 seeds
`scoring_logic_version` v2 and `weight_version` v2 with Momentum, Valuation and Quality each at weight
1. There is no sum-to-one constraint and `blend` normalises over the weights present, so 1:1:1 blends
to exactly a third each without storing a rounded 0.3333. Equal because any other split claims to
know which pillar predicts better, and nothing does yet: scores are logged daily precisely so a
backtest can set weights later. `reference()`'s guard, which today refuses a version that does not
weight Momentum positively, generalises to refusing one that weights none of the computed pillars
positively — the same empty-snapshot failure it exists to stop.

**D14 — Traceability by reproduction, with the period named.** For ratios, `fundamental_fact_id` stays
null, as it already is for momentum: one foreign key cannot hold sixteen facts (F8). The chain is
reproduction. Facts are append-only and the run records `as_of`, `cutoff_offset` and `git_sha`, so the
point-in-time read returns identical inputs and the ratio recomputes to its stored `raw_value`.
`period_end` records the basis end date and a new nullable column, `period_basis` (`'TTM'` or `'A'`),
records which basis, so a reader can see what period a yield describes without re-deriving it; both are
null for momentum. An inputs table was considered and rejected: at about 58 facts per security per
night it is 87,000 rows a night and 32 million a year, to hold a link reproduction already gives.

**D15 — The nightly gate does not widen.** `night.py`'s comment says the gate "widens to fundamentals"
when this cycle lands. It does not, and the comment is corrected. A wholly failed *prices* night is
dangerous because yesterday's bars make a wrong snapshot of *today*. A wholly failed *fundamentals*
night leaves yesterday's facts, which describe quarters that have not changed; D6's bounds already
make genuinely stale facts ineligible. Blocking scoring on a fundamentals failure would lose a correct
night to guard against a harmless one.

**D16 — The deploy is the switch.** The first night after migration 022 and the code deploy scores
under v2. `scoring_run.logic_version_id` separates the v1 nights from every one after, and `--as-of`
refuses a past date, so v1 history stands rather than being rewritten. `emits_alerts` stays false.

## 4. Conventions this follows

- Each package has a small public surface through `__init__.py`; nothing outside imports a submodule
  directly.
- Pure computation separated from database work: basis assembly and the formulas take plain values and
  return plain values, and only `run.py` and `peers.py` open a connection.
- A missing value is absent, never imputed and never zero.
- Constants that change a metric's meaning live in code and bump the logic version; weights live in the
  database.
- psycopg's `LiteralString` typing: no SQL assembled at runtime.
- Comments explain *why*, not *what*.

## 5. Layout

```
screener.scoring
  basis.py     pure: flow items onto one basis (TTM or annual), balance items at a date,
               the share-count guard and the currency rule -- facts in, dated inputs out
  ratios.py    pure: the ten formulas, the absence rules and the industry classes --
               dated inputs in, {code: (value, basis, period_end)} out
  pillars.py   `expected` becomes a per-security applicable count (D10)
  blend.py     `min_coverage` over weighted pillars, absent as 0 (D11)
  peers.py     the floor checked per (metric, peer group) bucket (D12)
  run.py       orchestration: one fact read, per-security computation, per-metric buckets,
               three pillars, one transaction; the weight guard generalised (D13)
  metrics.py   unchanged: momentum
```

**Migration 022** seeds the ten metrics (`is_input = false`; `higher_is_better` false only for
`debt_to_equity`; cadences per D3), `scoring_logic_version` v2, `weight_version` v2 with its three
`pillar_weight` rows, and adds `metric_daily.period_basis text check (period_basis in ('TTM', 'A'))`,
nullable. Adding a nullable column without a default to a partitioned table is a catalogue change and
rewrites no partition, though the inline check constraint still scans each partition once under a
brief lock, trivial at the current ~27k rows.

The read-only roles already hold `select` on `metric_daily` (013, 017, 018), so the console, Steven's
`sql` tool and the claude.ai connector see the new column with no grant.

## 6. A night

1. `reference()` reads logic v2, weight v2, the metric ids and each metric's pillar and direction.
2. Bars and corporate actions are read as today. Facts are read once, point in time under the run's
   cutoff, for every active security. Each security's level-2 industry code is read alongside its sector, for D4.
3. Per security: momentum as today; then `basis.py` assembles each ratio's inputs and `ratios.py`
   computes the applicable ratios, each with its basis and end date, or absent.
4. Values are bucketed by (metric, peer group). A bucket under `MIN_PEERS` moves to market level for
   that metric. Percentiles are computed within buckets, honouring `higher_is_better`.
5. Per security, up to three pillar scores, each with `expected` = its applicable count. `blend` writes
   the snapshot with `min_coverage` over all three weighted pillars.
6. All four derived tables are written in the one transaction, and one log line per metric records how
   many values were TTM, annual and absent.

## 7. Failure behaviour

- **The formulas never raise on the shape of the data.** Every zero, negative or missing denominator
  is an absence rule (D5), so bad data yields an absent value. Anything that does raise is a bug, and
  it fails the night exactly as today: the run is marked `failed`, nothing is half-written, and the
  date can be re-run (scoring, migration 020).
- **A security with no fundamentals is scored on Momentum** and written with `min_coverage = 0`
  (D11); it is not skipped. A security with nothing at all is skipped, as today (scoring D8).
- **A wholly failed fundamentals night scores normally** on the facts already held (D15).
- **Coverage is visible nightly.** The per-metric log line is where a Yahoo change — a series dropped,
  a sign convention flipped — shows up as a falling TTM count before anyone queries for it.

## 8. Testing

Pure tests carry the silent-wrong cases, because those are the ones a database test would pass through
without noticing.

**`basis.py`**
- Four consecutive quarters sum; the end date is the newest.
- **One quarter missing between four held yields no TTM** (F1).
- A newest quarter older than 135 days yields no TTM; an annual up to 456 days is used, and beyond it
  nothing.
- **One missing capex quarter moves the whole of `fcf_yield` to the annual basis**, not only capex.
- A balance item is read at a date under either period label.
- A fiscal Q4 and its year at one `period_end` stay distinct for flow items.
- The newest-date choice for `debt_to_equity` requires both items at the same date.
- **A split effective within the last 7 days makes market cap absent; one 7 or more days ago does
  not, and no split ever multiplies a share count** (F4).
- A fact in another currency is not an input.

**`ratios.py`**
- Each formula against a hand-computed fixture.
- **`fcf_yield` is OCF + capex, with capex negative**; a positive capex makes it absent.
- Negative equity makes `debt_to_equity`, `roe` and `book_yield` absent.
- Enterprise value ≤ 0 makes `ebitda_ev` absent; invested capital ≤ 0 makes `roic` absent.
- The tax clamp at 0, at 0.5, and at 0 for a pre-tax loss.
- A loss-maker's `earnings_yield` is negative and ranks below a profitable peer's.
- The classes: `banks-regional` yields `earnings_yield`, `book_yield` and `roe` and nothing else;
  `insurance-brokers` is standard; `reit-retail` yields `ffo_yield` and no `gross_margin` or
  `earnings_yield`.

**Database tests** (extending `tests/test_scoring_run.py`)
- A security with fundamentals writes three pillar rows.
- **A bank's Quality coverage is 1/1** (D10).
- **A security with no fundamentals has `min_coverage = 0`** and a blended score equal to its Momentum
  score (D11).
- **A bucket of eight falls back to market level** with `fallback_level = 0` (D12).
- `period_basis` and `period_end` are written for ratios and null for momentum.
- Under equal weights the blended score is the mean of the three pillar scores.
- `reference()` reads v2; a weight version weighting none of the computed pillars is refused.

**Against real data, before merge.** Run v2 over a full night's dump and check by hand:
- AAPL's `earnings_yield` equals its market cap over published TTM net income.
- APH, MNST, IESC, RUSHA and SFBS have market caps of the size in F4, not double.
- No sector has an empty Valuation or Quality bucket, and the per-metric TTM counts are within a few
  percent of F1's table.

## 10. Open parameters

- **135 / 80–100 / 456 days** (D6). The shape is settled; the values trade coverage against freshness
  and are cheap to move, at the cost of a logic-version bump.
- **The 7-day split window** (D8) rests on one observation: APH restated within six days. The nightly
  log will show whether a restatement ever arrives later than that — as a `shares_outstanding` fact for
  an old `period_end` observed more than a week after a split.
- **The tax clamp's 0.5 ceiling** (D3), which exists only to stop a one-off charge producing a tax rate
  above 100%.
- **`cash_and_equivalents` over `cash_and_short_term_investments`** (D3): 124 more securities covered,
  paid for with an overstated enterprise value where short-term investments are large.
- **`credit-services` and `capital-markets` as `standard`** (D4). If the screen shows banks misplaced
  there, the answer is an override list, and it should key on CIK as universe identity does.

## 12. Amendments from the implementation plan

Found while writing `docs/plans/2026-09-13-ratios.md`, before any code.

- **A1 — A ratio with no flow item has no basis.** `book_yield` and `debt_to_equity` read balance
  items only, so they write `period_basis = null`, with `period_end` the balance date. D14's column
  names the period for every ratio that assembles one.
- **A2 — The floor lives in `ranking.py`, and `pillars.py` is unchanged.** §5 placed the per-metric
  floor in `peers.py`, which opens a connection; ranking is pure. `score_pillar` already took
  `expected` as an argument, so D10 changed its caller.
- **A3 — Market fallback ranks against every producer.** v1's `resolve` sent a thin sector's members to
  the `market` group but ranked them only among the others that also fell, so a thin sector was
  ranked against itself under a label saying otherwise. It was never exercised; D12 would have been
  the first to exercise it.
- **A4 — `debt_to_equity`'s two-step date rule is one step.** The newest date ≤ 135 days, else the
  newest ≤ 456, always selects the newest date ≤ 456 at which both items are held.
- **A5 — A stale close gives no market cap.** Market cap is absent when the latest visible close
  is more than 7 days before `as_of` (`CLOSE_MAX_AGE_DAYS`). D8 bounded the split window but not
  the close itself, which could be 13 months old: bars that stopped before a split pair a
  pre-split close with a restated share count; a failing price ingest also writes no split rows,
  so the window never triggers; and a halted security still active would be valued on an old
  price. Found in the whole-branch review, before any production night under v2. Momentum's own
  `latest` close in `metrics.compute` has the same unbounded shape; it predates this cycle and is
  left to a later one.
- **E1 — §8's TTM comparison was against the wrong population.** "Within a few percent of F1's
  table" compared a ratio's count with a line item's count over the whole universe. A ratio covers
  only the classes it applies to and needs every input on the same quarters. Verified on
  production on 2026-09-13: `earnings_yield` 1,325 TTM = clean `net_income` TTM over standard and
  balance-sheet securities (1,182 + 143) exactly; `interest_cover` 1,040 against 1,043 securities
  with clean joint `ebit` and `interest_expense` TTM over standard and REIT securities, the
  difference being the `interest_expense ≤ 0` absence rule.
- **E2 — v2's Momentum includes the split fix.** Found while starting the UI swap, after v2 merged
  and before its first production night: momentum double-counted every split in its window (scoring
  spec D4 erratum). The fix changes what Momentum means, but no v2 night had been scored, so it ships
  inside v2 rather than as v3 — the reasoning A5 used. v1's four nights keep the distorted values.
